01

Respiratory season, arriving early and hard

Δ24h ↑ ESCALATING
Class: ongoing deaths Horizon: now through February Arrives through: lungs, schools, wards Exposure weighting effect: none

What changed in 24h

The season’s shape is now measurable, and it is early. CDC surveillance for the week ending Oct 24 (published Oct 30) shows flu hospitalisation rates at their highest for this point in the season since 2010–11, activity running four to five weeks ahead of last year, 98.9% of subtyped influenza A viruses H1N1, the season’s first paediatric death confirmed, and CDC estimates of at least 1,000,000 illnesses, 16,000 hospitalisations and 900 deaths already this season. Forecasts project 4,800 to 23,200 flu admissions in a single week by mid-November.

Why #1: ranked on certainty, and it is not close

This is the one pathway on the board that is guaranteed to reach ordinary homes this winter, because it already has. It needs no escalation, no breakthrough, no adversary: it arrives through schools, wards, workplaces and family gatherings, and it kills mostly at the edges of age, the very young and the old. Every other candidate on this page requires something unusual to happen. This one requires nothing.

Watch triggers

Hospital capacity strain in major metros · a strain shift away from H1N1 · vaccine-match failure · RSV and COVID curves rising simultaneously instead of in sequence.

Sources: CDC FluView week 42 via Contemporary Pediatrics (medical trade press, reporting CDC data) · regional surveillance roundups, Nov 2 (news reports).
02

Arctic outbreak into a strained winter grid

Δ24h ↑ mild, WATCHING
Class: seasonal tail risk Horizon: late November through February Arrives through: walls, pipes, wires Exposure weighting effect: ↑ raised

What changed in 24h

Forecasters reported Nov 3 that the stratospheric polar vortex is heading for a disruption that could be the earliest on record, with winds possibly reversing around Nov 8 and 96% of European model members showing reversal by Nov 11; the vortex may split, with one core over North America and surface cold following weeks later, into late November and December. The counterweight is real: a very strong El Niño favours a mild northern winter, and the official outlook still leans warm. The honest marker is mild ↑: the setup for the cold scenario strengthened; the cold itself has not arrived.

Why #2: the hometown catastrophe with a precedent

Cold alone rarely kills at scale. Cold plus power failure does: in February 2021, Winter Storm Uri forced 20,000 MW of rolling blackouts in Texas and 246 people died, most from hypothermia and its knock-on failures, in a state far warmer than the northern tier. Northern cities run colder, on older housing, and their grid margins are thinning: NERC’s long-term assessment projects the PJM interconnection’s reserve margin falling from 29.7% (2026) toward 13.9% (2030) as demand grows faster than capacity. The pathway is concrete: outbreak, demand spike, gas freeze-offs, emergency, homes going cold room by room.

Watch triggers

Wind reversal confirmed (expected ~Nov 8) · cold anomalies appearing in the CPC hazards outlook for late November · any grid emergency declaration during an outbreak · NERC’s 2026–27 winter assessment, due Nov 18.

Sources: FOX Weather, Nov 3 and Nature World News, Nov 3 (news reports of forecast models) · NERC reliability assessments via trade summaries (regulator documents) · Texas DSHS Uri death count via the FERC/NERC review.
03

Summer heat dome over the city, grid as the hinge

Δ24h → WATCHING
Class: ongoing / recurring, seasonal Horizon: June–August 2027 Arrives through: air conditioners, and their absence Exposure weighting effect: none

What changed in 24h

The same El Niño that argues for a mild winter is loading the far side of the year: Carbon Brief’s Nov 3 update projects 2027 at about 1.74°C above pre-industrial with a 96% chance of being the warmest year on record. On this board the planetary number only matters translated: it raises the odds that next summer delivers a multi-day heat dome to northern cities whose housing and grid were built for a colder century.

Why #3: this exact event has already happened in a city like yours

In July 1995 a heat wave killed more than 700 people in a single northern city in under a week, most of them elderly, alone, in upper-floor apartments, many with air conditioning they could not afford to run or that failed under load. It remains the template: heat kills in cities when the dome parks, the nights stop cooling, and the grid or the household budget gives out. No new technology, adversary or anomaly is required; only a hotter baseline, which is precisely what the Nov 3 numbers describe.

Watch triggers

Spring grid reserve assessments for summer 2027 · El Niño decaying slower than forecast (a hotter summer signal) · cooling-assistance funding changes · the first multi-day dome forecast of the season.

Sources: Carbon Brief, Nov 3 (specialist analysis) · 1995 event: historical public-health record.

Method, in brief

Rankings weight each risk by how directly it can reach the readership, how fast it could move, and whether it can be stopped; the audience itself is never the subject of this page. Those weighting factors are fixed in advance and do not change with the day’s news. THE REACH TEST: to rank, a pathway must name the ordinary system it arrives through (lungs, walls, wires, water) and either be measurably underway or have killed in comparable cities before. Spectacle without a mechanism does not rank, however severe. Exposure is assessed on four dimensions: direct exposure (could the pathway physically reach the population assessed), indirect exposure (through military, economic, infrastructure, or environmental channels), scale, and time horizon. Likelihood uses four defined bands only, Very unlikely / Unlikely / Plausible / Likely, over a 12-month horizon. No invented percentages; other people’s numbers are attributed to them and flagged when they answer a different question. Confidence describes the evidence, never the prose. A “no material change” day is a legitimate edition. Method and format were pressure-tested before launch with two independent AI systems, one auditing the ranking logic and one the product design, and revised where they pushed back. Full source register and corrections log ship with each edition.

Exemplars this format learned from: X-Risk Daily (daily, many stories, forecaster-led; we differ by ranking only three, and by keeping the ledger), the Bulletin’s Doomsday Clock (one annual symbol; we are daily and evidence-first), Sentinel Global Risks Watch (probabilities we cite, never blend into our own score), and the Lancet Commission on 21st Century Global Threats.
