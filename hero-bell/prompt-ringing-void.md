# Ringing bell in a black void — generation prompt (fresh T2I, no edits, no references)

SUBJECT AND SCALE. One bell, and only one bell, in an absolute black void. This bell is not large, it is gigantic: a colossal cast bell of overwhelming magnitude, a mass so great the image strains to contain it, a monument of iron with the presence of a planet. It must loom. It must dominate the frame the way a mountain dominates a valley. The viewer is very small before it, and close, far too close, inside the radius of its power. Nothing else exists in this universe: no floor, no ground, no horizon, no pedestal, no chains worth noticing, no architecture, no landscape, no reflections, no ash, no debris. Only the bell, and what the bell is doing to the nothing around it.

CAMERA AND ACTION. This is an intense action shot, not a portrait and not an emblem. Place the camera at an unusual, unstable angle: from the front and from below and from the side at once, a low three-quarter worm's-eye view with a dutch tilt, as if the photographer were kneeling directly under the swing. The bell is caught mid-ring at the most violent instant of its arc, heaving diagonally through the frame, mouth flaring wide open toward the lens, clapper hurled to the extremity of its travel and about to strike. There must be motion in the mass itself: a sense of unstoppable momentum, of tons of iron moving faster than iron should move, the whole image leaning with the swing. Freeze that one catastrophic millisecond with total clarity.

HEAT AND POWER. The bell does not merely glow; it burns with magnitude. Its heat is a physical force in the picture. The dark iron is unevenly incandescent: molten veins of orange and white-hot yellow crackle across the crown and down one flank like lightning frozen in metal, the inside of the mouth is a furnace of napalm-bright liquid fire, and along the rim run glints of napalm, that sticky, clinging, impossible-to-extinguish brightness that eats the dark. Other parts of the body stay cold, black, ancient iron, and that contrast is essential: the heat must be uneven, alive, distributed like weather across the surface, never a uniform glow and never confined to the base. Embers shear off the moving mass and streak past the camera. Sparks drip from the clapper. The bell radiates the kind of power that has no interest in being observed; it is indifferent to the viewer the way a star is indifferent, and its magnitude is the entire mood.

THE WAVES. The ringing is visible, and it is the second subject of the image. Waves of heat distortion pour off the bell and travel outward through the void, and these waves must conform to the shape of the bell: every wavefront is an expanding echo of the bell's own silhouette, its rounded crown, its swelling shoulders, the dramatic flare of its skirt and mouth, drawn again and again in warped, superheated air, each front larger than the last, each one a portrait of the bell at a greater distance. Near the bell they are tight, bright and faithful, hugging its contours. As they travel they grow more energetic and more uneven: they bulge and collapse asymmetrically, their spacing is irregular, they tear and fray into plasma and smoke, they lens and bend the blackness behind them as if spacetime itself were buckling under the magnitude of the sound. This is distortion of space and time rendered visible: the void ripples in the shape of its cause. The waves are not circles, not rings on water, not a neat spiral; they are the bell's silhouette, repeated by physics, breaking down with distance.

LIGHT, TEXTURE AND EXCLUSIONS. Light comes only from the bell. Its fire must model its own hammered, pitted, ancient iron: every dent and casting flaw lit from within or raked by its own burning. Photorealistic, cinematic, extreme contrast against perfect black. No people, no hands, no text, no letters, no watermark, no border, no vignette decoration. Nothing sits on anything. Nothing recedes into a distance. There is a bell, there is its heat, there is the shaped disturbance of the void, and there is power.
